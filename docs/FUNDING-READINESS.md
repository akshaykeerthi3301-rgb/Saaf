## 1. The verdict, in one paragraph

**Not ready for investors. Ready for government money — once three paperwork steps are done.** What exists is an unusually well-built product with almost no market evidence behind it. Investors fund evidence (people using it, coming back, and paying), not engineering. Government schemes fund the *stage you are actually at* — a working prototype with a clear idea — and ask for paperwork, not traction. So the right order is: paperwork → grants → real users → angels/seed. Pitching venture capital now would likely end in a polite "come back with numbers", and a bad first pitch is remembered.

## 3. What money actually exists (OBSERVED 2026-10-16 unless tagged)

**Kerala Startup Mission (KSUM) — innovation grants** (startupmission.kerala.gov.in, grants.startupmission.in):

| Grant | Up to | Stage it fits | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Idea Grant** | ₹3 lakh | Ideation / design / proof of concept | Innovator based in Kerala; incorporation (Pvt Ltd/LLP/OPC) and a KSUM Unique ID before money is paid; "innovative technology product" only |
| **Productisation Grant** | ₹7 lakh (₹12 lakh if women hold the majority) | Prototype → market-ready product — **this is you** | Pvt Ltd/LLP active on MCA; KSUM Unique ID at the time of filing; **you must put in 20% of the grant as margin money**; no founder salary, rent, or software over ₹2 lakh |
| **Market Acceleration Grant** | ₹10 lakh | Early revenue | Needs revenue; a Goodwill Fund commitment (give back at least the grant amount once you are doing well) |
| **Scale-up Grant** | ₹15 lakh | ₹10 lakh revenue in 6 months, **or** ₹30 lakh raised | Same goodwill commitment |

All four together are capped at ₹15 lakh. Applications open in calls ("Kerala Innovation Drive" each year); you pitch to an expert committee judged on team, product innovation, marketing, financials and your fund-use plan.

**Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS)** (seedfund.startupindia.gov.in): up to **₹20 lakh grant** for proof of concept / prototype / product trials, and up to **₹50 lakh** as convertible debentures or debt for market entry and scaling. **DPIIT-recognised and incorporated less than 2 years ago** at the time you apply; **individuals cannot apply**; it is paid *through an approved incubator*, you may name three; no application fee; incubators decide within 45 days; if rejected wait 3 months. The two-year clock is why paperwork should start now.

**DPIIT recognition** (free; startupindia.gov.in): Pvt Ltd, LLP or partnership; turnover under ₹200 crore; up to 10 years old. It unlocks SISFS and the tax holiday (3 years of income-tax exemption within the first 10 — needs a further approval, **CLAIMED** here). The "angel tax" was abolished for all unlisted companies from FY 2025-26 (**CLAIMED**, secondary sources — confirm with your CA). The ₹10,000 crore Fund of Funds invests *through venture funds*, not directly in you.

**Not verified — worth a call but I have not read their terms:** NIDHI-PRAYAS and NIDHI-EIR (Department of Science and Technology), TIDE 2.0 (MeitY), the Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups, Kerala angel networks and KSIDC. Do not plan around numbers I have not seen.

**Realistic non-dilutive total over about 18 months, if it goes well:** Idea ₹3L + Productisation ₹7L + SISFS ₹20L ≈ **₹30 lakh**, up to ~₹80 lakh counting the SISFS debenture. Enough for a year of lean runway and a first hire — not a Series A.

## 4. The plan

**Weeks 0–4 — paperwork (nothing else matters until this is done)**
1. Incorporate a **Private Limited** (cleaner for investors than an LLP). Kerala-registered if you want the KSUM schemes. Founder equity vesting from day one.
2. **DPIIT recognition** the same week. 3. **KSUM Unique ID.** 4. A **lawyer's one-page opinion** on the data sources and the DPDP Act: what Jobinko reads, what it stores, what "Apply for me" does. ₹10–25k well spent — it is the most common reason diligence stalls.
5. Move hosting to paid plans (~$45/month) so a paid plan is lawful to advertise.

**Weeks 4–12 — evidence**
6. **Choose the wedge.** My recommendation, because the data already points there: **MBA / early-career and management candidates** — your index now carries PwC, Barclays, Morgan Stanley, Mastercard, Amazon and 2,000 more, and "every score shows the line from the posting" is exactly what that candidate lacks.
7. **Get 100 real users, then 500.** Kerala gives you a natural route: placement cells at IIM Kozhikode, NIT Calicut, CUSAT, TKM and the engineering/MBA colleges; Technopark and Infopark communities; alumni groups. One placement-cell partnership beats a thousand ad impressions.
8. **Measure three numbers weekly:** activation (uploaded a résumé *and* opened a scored job), week-4 retention, and **outcome** — of users who applied to a job we scored high, how many got an interview. That outcome number is your only real moat; start recording it now.
9. **Get one paying pilot** — a college placement cell paying for a student licence, or ten users on a ₹199–499 plan. The amount does not matter; "someone paid" changes every conversation.

**Months 3–6 — the money**
10. **KSUM Productisation Grant** (and the Idea Grant if you have not used it). 11. **SISFS** through a Kerala incubator (KSUM's own incubation, or an approved incubator near you — check the portal's list). 12. Only then **angels** — with real numbers. Seed capital from VCs is a month-12+ conversation.

## 5. The pitch, in plain words

- **One line:** "Jobinko shows every Indian job-seeker which open roles they can actually win and the exact line in the posting that says why — no ghost jobs, no guessing."
- **The problem:** applicants send hundreds of applications blind; portals rank by who pays, not by fit; ghost postings waste weeks.
- **Why you:** you lived the search; you built a pipeline reading ~2,000 company career sites; every number on the site is checkable (`/audit`).
- **The honest ask:** a grant to turn a working prototype into a product 1,000 people use, with named milestones (users, retention, first paying pilot) and a margin-money line.
- **What you must not say:** "we have 43,000 jobs" as the headline (everyone says that); "AI matching" (yours is rule-based, which is a *feature* — say so); anything about LinkedIn/Naukri data (you do not use it, and that is correct).

## 6. Risks I would name before an investor does

1. **Legal on sources** (above). 2. **Job seekers do not pay** — in India the buyer is usually the employer, the college, or a coaching brand; your paid plan needs a payer who is not the candidate. 3. **Distribution** — the best product loses to the one students already use. 4. **Single point of failure** — the system was built mostly by an AI assistant and one founder; write down how you would keep it running if a tool changed. 5. **Auto-apply** — it can get candidates banned by employers; keep it off by default, as it is.

You told me: **no registered company · based in Kerala · no co-founder or advisor · 5–8 real users besides you · no college or community chosen yet.** Honest reading: you are at the very start of the funding road, and every answer is fixable in weeks except the second one, which is already a point in your favour — Kerala has the most founder-friendly state startup machinery in India.

**What this changes**

1. **You can apply for the KSUM Idea Grant before you have a company** — incorporation and the KSUM ID are needed only before the money is paid (OBSERVED). The catch: that grant is for "ideation / design / proof of concept", and Jobinko is further along than that. Frame it truthfully as *validating the product with real users* (and say so in the pitch); if KSUM considers you past that stage, the Productisation Grant (₹7 lakh) is the right door, but it needs the company and the KSUM ID first.
2. **The company form is your first real decision, because you have no co-founder.** My understanding — **ask a CA to confirm** — is that a Private Limited needs two directors and two shareholders; a One Person Company lets one person own it and can be converted later; an LLP needs two partners. KSUM accepts Pvt Ltd, LLP and OPC for the Idea Grant; the Productisation and later grants name Pvt Ltd or LLP. DPIIT's page lists Private Limited, Partnership, LLP and Cooperative — **whether an OPC qualifies for DPIIT recognition is unverified; ask.** The cleanest route is usually a Private Limited with a trusted second director holding a small stake.
3. **No co-founder or advisor is the weakest part of the story.** Investors and KSUM's committee score "team" first. Cheapest fixes: ask KSUM for a mentor at the Idea Day, find one technical advisor who can say yes to "could this run without the founder's AI assistant for a month?", and look for a co-founder with sales or campus distribution rather than another builder. If you give advisory shares, keep them small and vesting (typically well under 1% each) — confirm the numbers with your CA.
4. **5–8 users is a starting line, not traction.** The next number to chase is 100 people who each upload a résumé, then 500. Keep a list of the 5–8 now: what they used, what they said, whether they came back. Those quotes are your first pitch slide.
5. **"No college chosen" is the cheapest problem to solve.** KSUM's own guidelines route student grants through college innovation centres (IEDCs). Pick **one** target this week from: the placement cell of an MBA programme near you (IIM Kozhikode or a Kerala B-school), the placement cell of an engineering college with a strong IEDC, or a Technopark/Infopark community. Offer them something concrete: "a free scored shortlist and a résumé check for your final-year batch, 30 minutes in a hall".
